\section{Prospective Conflict and Cyclicity Stress Tests}
\label{app:dataset_stress_test}
This section is a preregistered extension template; dashes denote unmeasured quantities, not zero. It does not change the completed UF experiments. Selection uses development data and measured cost before final-test access. A negative screening result is retained.

\paragraph{Separate the mechanisms.}
Within-objective cycles test preference representation and opponent adaptation; cross-objective disagreement tests compromise selection. Neither guarantees a Nash advantage over other game-based methods. We first measure stable cycles, objective conflict, joint strict feasibility, and separation of exact targets. A large raw cycle count alone is insufficient: it increases with the number of candidate triples and can involve only weak responses.

\paragraph{Dataset contract.}
We screen fresh responses on SafeRLHF prompts under fixed helpfulness and harmlessness rubrics, and WildChecklists prompts under their native checklist items. PROSPER uses the latter with prompt-dependent objectives \citep{zhang2026prosper}. Native checklist positions are not shared objective identities and cannot be substituted for the global $k$ in NBPO. WildChecklists fine-tuning requires a separately frozen set of semantically constant global rubrics; such feedback is an adaptation. Existing SafeRLHF pair labels support a conflict audit but do not by themselves supply complete response triangles. New LLM judgments are AI feedback, not new human annotations.

\paragraph{Screening.}
Use $200$ development prompts per new source and eight responses per prompt. On the same $28$ unordered pairs, collect both orders and two independent repetitions per order; use a preselected $50$-prompt subset for five additional judgments per order. Report normalized cyclic-triangle rates, repeatability, no-Condorcet rates, order sensitivity, and a fitted-BT noise control. Estimate objective conflict on the same non-tied pairs. Report prompt-level variation rather than pooling checklist positions as global parties. Table~\ref{tab:dataset_readiness} is a compact index; per-objective denominators and uncertainty remain in the accompanying result artifact.

\paragraph{Factorial mechanism control.}
Cross low/high cyclicity with low/high objective conflict using matched finite games, fixed payoff scales and reference distributions. Record realized factors and the joint-feasibility margin; do not select games by method ranking. Evaluate exact targets before fitting a policy. In Table~\ref{tab:factorial_game}, $\Delta M$ is the NBPO-minus-fixed-reference difference in normalized worst surplus under the same adaptive evaluator. TV compares NBPO with utilitarian targets. These constructed games identify mechanisms and are not evidence of naturally cyclic human preferences; no cell is required to favor NBPO.

\paragraph{One confirmatory policy panel.}
Select at most one compatible feedback contract, with $2{,}000/500/1{,}000$ disjoint train/development/test prompts (a cost-based reduction is frozen before outcome inspection). Keep the completed UF panel as a transitive-supervision control. All new methods share source pools, feedback, base initialization, token accounting, and a declared tuning budget. The default two objectives are helpfulness and harmlessness; changing them requires a separately named contract. The full test distribution is primary; a conflict stratum defined before policy evaluation is secondary. Seeds and missing-data coverage cannot be silently reduced. BT-projected Nash is a projection control, not a reproduction of a globally trained reward model.
