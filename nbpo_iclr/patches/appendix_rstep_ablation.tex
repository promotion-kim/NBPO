\subsection{The alternating inner map, and why it is an ablation}
\label{app:rstep-ablation}

Earlier versions of this method solved \eqref{eq:weighted-policy} by applying the
fixed-opponent update \eqref{eq:fixed-opponent-update} $R$ times per dual step.
That map is exactly mirror ascent at unit step size on the same concave objective
$J_\lambda$, so it contracts only while $\eta_t\sum_k\lambda_kQ_k$ remains small.
The raw multipliers satisfy $\lambda_k=1/s_k$ and therefore diverge as a surplus
approaches zero, at which point the exponent saturates the per-prompt softmax and
the iteration oscillates between vertices instead of converging.

On the feasibility-preserving controlled benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, with $\rho^\star$ held fixed while Bradley--Terry
deviance rises:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & $R$-step map & direct inner solve \\
\midrule
cells converged & $0/25$ & $25/25$ \\
fixed-point residual at $\alpha=1$ & $1.000$ & $\le10^{-4}$ \\
normalized min surplus at $\alpha=1$ & $-3.373$ & $+0.931$ \\
raw multiplier norm & $608$ & $30$--$42$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Damping does not repair it. At $R=150$ with damping $0.9$ the last-iterate change
falls to $5.4\times10^{-2}$ while a single \emph{undamped} application of the map
still moves the iterate by $0.54$: the iteration is crawling, not converging.

We report this map only as an approximation and failure ablation. It is never a
performance arm, and the released implementation refuses any final configuration
that selects it or that consumes a target produced by it.
